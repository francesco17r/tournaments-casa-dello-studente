Rules

These apply to all three tournaments. If there is any doubt or dispute, the organiser decides.

General rules

  • Only residents of Casa dello Studente can take part. Entry is free.
  • Privacy: your personal details, including your room number, are not made public. The site shows only your team name, or just your name if you don't use a team name.
  • You can play in more than one tournament: fill in the sign-up form once for each tournament you want to join.
  • After the draw, opponents agree on a day and time for their match between themselves.
  • Matches are played in the common areas during the hours allowed by the residence. Don't hold the tables longer than needed, and leave them tidy.
  • Right after the match, both players send the result to the organiser, or one of them sends a photo of the score.
  • If someone doesn't show up or doesn't reply before the deadline, they lose by forfeit (table football and ping pong 2–0, chess 2–0). If neither player got in touch, the organiser decides.
  • Fair play first: we're here to have fun.

Formats and standings

  • Ping pong: 4 groups of 5 players, everyone plays everyone in their group. A match is best of 3 sets and is worth 1 point to the winner, 0 to the loser. The top 2 of each group go through to the knockout stage.
  • Table football: one league with all the teams. Each team plays 4 matches against opponents drawn at random by the organiser. A match is best of 3 games and is worth 1 point to the winner. The top 4 teams go through to the knockout stage (semi-finals and final).
  • Chess: 4 groups (2 of 4 players, 2 of 5), drawn at random. Everyone plays 3 matches against players of their own group. In each group of 5 the numbers don't add up, so one player plays only 2 matches and gets a bye, which counts as a match won (2 points); the byes go to the first players who signed up. A match is two games, one with white and one with black: each game is worth 1 point for a win, ½ for a draw and 0 for a loss, so a match is worth 0 to 2 points. The top 2 of each group go through to the knockout stage (quarter-finals). In the chess groups, after head-to-head, the number of matches won decides.
  • If players or teams are level on points, the head-to-head result decides (when two are tied and have played each other), then the difference between games or sets won and lost, then games or sets won.
  • In the knockout stage every match is played like in the group stage. In the ping pong and chess brackets the quarter-finals are fixed: 1st A vs 2nd B, 1st C vs 2nd D, 1st B vs 2nd A, 1st D vs 2nd C. Two players from the same group can meet again only in the final. The bracket is shown on each game's page.

Table football

  • 2 vs 2, with the same partner for the whole tournament.
  • A game is won by the first team to score 10 goals. A match is best of 3 games.
  • No spinning the rods 360°.
  • Goals from any rod count.
  • After each goal, the team that conceded puts the ball back in play. Partners can swap positions only after a goal.

Ping pong

  • Singles. A match is best of 3 sets.
  • Each set goes to 21 points, with a 2-point lead.
  • Five serves each. From 20–20, players alternate one serve each.
  • If a serve touches the net and lands in the right half, it is replayed.
  • Players switch ends after each set.

Chess

  • 10 minutes each + 1 second per move, using a chess clock app on a phone.
  • Each match is two games. Colours for the first game are drawn, then swapped for the second. Touch a piece, move that piece.
  • A player who runs out of time loses, unless the opponent has no pieces left that could checkmate: then it's a draw.
  • No phones or other help during the game, apart from the clock.
  • In the group stage a level match (1–1) stays level. In the knockout stage, if the match is level after two games, a tiebreak game of 5 minutes + 3 seconds is played, with colours drawn. If that is drawn too, another one is played with colours reversed, until someone wins.
